# Project Conventions

Enforce on EVERY change — and when fixing a gap, sweep the whole class of it project-wide, not just the reported instance.

**Authorization model — the store is an organization (docs/STORE-ORGANIZATION-SPEC.md is the source of truth):** power comes from **membership** alone — a `store_user` row `(user_id, store_id, role_id)`, one role per person per store — never from who created whom. Nobody owns an account: there is no `users.created_by`, no admin sets or knows another person's password, and a person's name, phone, email and password change only from their own Profile or "forgot password". Two tiers, mutually exclusive — a platform account is never a store member and a store member never holds a platform role (accepting an invitation, the owner email of a new store and platform invitations all refuse the other tier):
- **Platform** = a membership on the sentinel `store_id = 0` holding a global role (`User::globalRole()`). It works above the stores — every store, every account — limited to its role's permissions. **Super-Admin** is the role named `Super-Admin` there. **A super admin holds every permission** (owner's rule, 2026-09-16: "sab matlab sab"): a `Gate::before` in `AppServiceProvider` answers yes to every permission check whatever the role's rows say — a permission made later, or renamed, included. It answers permission checks only: rules that are not permissions still stand (the primary's protection, Super-Admin never changed and the Owner role never deleted, a store's last Owner, a super admin never deleting themselves), and `network-ads-toggle` — a place ("inside a shop, logged in as one of its people"), not a permission — keeps its own test. Only super admins make the store roles and the platform roles (see **Roles**), look after every store's custom roles from the platform (the Roles page lists them in a card of their own), put anybody in any store with a role, change that role or take them out (Users page → **Stores**, owner's rule 2026-09-17: `UserController::storeAccess`/`assignToStore`/`changeStoreRole`/`removeFromStore` — straight in, nobody invited; a role that store has; never a platform account; the store keeps an Owner; taking someone out asks for the password), invite platform staff, remove a platform role, "Log in as" and reach the permission catalogue. **"Log in as"** keeps two session keys (`ImpersonateController::SESSION_KEYS`: who to return to, and whom they view as), written after the impersonation's own login; every sign-in clears them (a `Login` listener in `AppServiceProvider`) and "stop" works only for the account being viewed as — a session whose impersonated account was deleted mid-way once handed the super admin to the next person who signed in on that browser. The **primary** super admin (`User::primarySuperAdminId()`, the first Super-Admin membership) alone may take Super-Admin from someone or delete a super admin account, and nobody may act on the primary.
- **Store** = memberships in real stores. Permissions are read against the membership of `session('current_store_id')` (`User::contextPermissionNames()`; a platform role always wins over a stale store left in the session). Holding a permission in store A grants nothing in store B. A store's role may also carry the platform permissions that can work inside one store, and there they reach that store alone (see **Platform permissions**).

**A role does not matter — its permissions do (owner's rule, 2026-09-17: "role matter nahi karta, permission matter karta ha"):** whatever a role is called, what its holders may do is exactly what its permissions allow, and a page shows exactly those buttons and links. No rule asks "is this person an Owner?" to allow an action; the Owner role's only special standing is below. The super admin decides who holds which permission and takes care that a role gets nothing that could break a store.

**Roles — no built-in roles but Super-Admin (owner's rules, 2026-09-17):** the super admin makes a role and says what it is for. **Store roles** (`is_global = false`, `store_id` NULL) are made on the platform's Roles page with "Store role" and are offered in EVERY store; the super admin renames them and changes what they allow (every store at once, `RoleController::update`); inside a store they are read-only. **Delete** is offered on every role but Super-Admin and the Owner role — a role somebody still holds is refused with **"Please unassign {role} from everyone first: {n} person still holds it."** (the Roles page says it as a toast at once, before any password; `RoleController::destroy` refuses it again with a 422). **The Owner role** is the store role marked `roles.key = 'owner'` (`Role::OWNER`, `Role::owner()`, `Role::isOwner()` — never found by its name): its holders are the store's owners — a store may have several (partners) — "at least one Owner" counts it, public signup, owner invitations and Create store give it, and a store changes hands by giving it on the Members page. It is renamed and changed like any store role but never deleted. **Platform** roles (`is_global = true`) are made with "Platform role", for the team above the stores. What a role is for is fixed once it exists. **Custom** roles (`store_id` = the store) are a store's own, made on its Roles page: any member holding `role-update`/`role-destroy` manages them within reach whoever created them, a super admin manages every one from the platform, they survive their creator, and one still held cannot be deleted. `Role::STARTERS` (Owner, Admin, Staff, Viewer — keys `owner`/`admin`/`staff`/`viewer`) is only where an installation begins: one migration inserts the permission catalogue and all four roles (`2026_09_16_110200_insert_permissions_and_starter_roles`) and the seeder only puts the Owner role back should it be missing — neither ever touches an existing role, so a re-seed cannot undo the super admin's changes or bring back a role they deleted; a starter key gives no power. Owner and Admin start with every store permission and View Stores; Delete Stores starts with the Owner role alone. A name must be new to every list the role is shown in (`RoleController::validated`), compared with accents, case, spacing and punctuation folded away: a store role against every store role and every store's custom roles (it is listed in all of them), a custom role against its store's list, a platform role against the platform roles, and nothing may be named like Super-Admin. A role of another store is never usable here (`Role::availableInStore`: 422 on `role_id` when inviting, assigning or changing a role, 404 on the Roles endpoints). (The migrations that upgraded the owner's own database to this model — the people conversion, the merge of same-named store roles, the store-transfer permission and its removal — were squashed away on 2026-09-17, once only that one database had ever run them: `database/migrations` now builds the final schema and the starter data directly.) **The Super-Admin anchor is the exact name compared in PHP (`Role::SUPER_ADMIN`, `Role::superAdminId()`, `User::isSuperAdmin()`), never a `WHERE name = 'Super-Admin'`:** MySQL's `utf8mb4_0900_ai_ci` collation reads "Súper-Admin" as that very name, so a SQL comparison let a look-alike custom role make every holder a super admin.

**Hierarchy (one place: `App\Services\StoreTeam`):** to **assign** a role (invite, change role) it must be available in the store and the actor must hold every permission of it in this store (`mayAssign`). To **manage** an existing member (change role, remove) it must not be yourself and the actor must hold every permission of the member's current role (`mayManage`). Both compare permissions only (`roleIsWithinReach`) — the Owner role is given and managed like any other, by whoever holds everything it allows (owner's choice, 2026-09-17). Because an Owner can be reached by somebody who is not one, the last-Owner rule below is checked on its own in every change or removal of a member (Members page `MemberController::update`/`refuseTheLastOwner`, Users → Stores), and a row never offers it (`can_manage` is false on a store's only Owner). **At least one Owner, always:** the last Owner cannot be demoted, removed, leave, or delete their account — and every change that could break that is decided under the store row's lock (`StoreTeam::changeTeam`, `lockStoresOwnedBy`), reading the owners INSIDE the lock, so two co-owners acting at the same moment cannot both pass. **Leaving** is open to every member: from the Members page, from "Your stores" on Settings → Stores, or — without View Stores where they work — from "Your stores" on the profile (`ProfileController::leaveStore` returns to the page it came from). A store changes hands **on the Members page** (owner's rule, 2026-09-17 — there is no Transfer Ownership and no `store-transfer` permission any more): whoever holds everything the Owner role allows gives it to another member with Change role, and the new Owner may then change the first one's role — the last-Owner rule keeps the store from being left without one in between. The server sends what each row allows (`can_manage`, `assignable_roles`, `can`), so a page never offers what the controller would refuse — and the controller checks it again regardless.

**How people enter (nobody is created by somebody else):**
- **Public signup** (`/register`, routes/auth.php): a customer creates their account and their store in one form and becomes its **Owner** — server-chosen (`Role::owner()`), never client input.
- **Invitation** — from a store's Members page (`member-invite`); from the platform when it creates a store for a customer (`store-store`: the owner's email gets an Owner invitation) or gives an ownerless store an owner; and for platform staff (super admins). The 64-character token lives only in the email — the database keeps its SHA-256 hash (`invitations.token_hash`) — the link lasts `Invitation::LIFETIME_DAYS` (7), Resend rotates the token and Revoke deletes it. The link's page (`InvitationResponseController`) creates the account when none exists (marked verified: opening the link proved the inbox) and otherwise makes the person sign in first — **the link alone never logs anybody in** — and tells a person signed in with another email so. Accepting and registering take the invitation row under a lock, so a double submit or a second tab never uses one link twice. The email goes out through `Invitation::sendLink()`, which reports a refusing mail server instead of throwing — the response says `email_sent: false` and the page shows it as an error, while the invitation stands for Resend. On the platform, **"Invite owner" is offered only for a store with no Owner** (owner's rule, 2026-09-17): the row's `can.invite_owner` needs `store-store` and an `owners_count` of 0, and `StoreController::inviteOwner` refuses a store that has one (422, "{store} already has an Owner.") — more Owners come from the store's own Members page, or Users → Stores. A member of that store is made Owner at once; anybody else is invited. The same address again sends a fresh link (expired or not), and a different address — or a member made Owner — retires the earlier owner invitations (`retireOwnerInvitations`), so a mistyped email stops working. Only the primary super admin invites a new super admin (the one who can also take it away). Both sides are throttled by named limiters (`invitations`, `invitation-response`). The dashboard deliberately lists no invitations to accept: public signup does not verify an email, so only the emailed link may accept.
- **No API:** this is a web-only app — there is no `routes/api.php` and no token API, by owner decision (a half-built mobile login was deleted 2026-09-03). Do not add API routes without explicit instruction; any future API needs validation, throttling, and tests from day one.

**Visibility & scoping:** inside a store, `member-view` shows **every** member of the current store (the viewer marked "You") and its pending invitations — never anybody outside it, never a platform account — a store's people have no Accounts page of their own, the accounts pages are the platform's alone (`can:global-tier`, owner's rule 2026-09-17); `store-view` on a store's role opens the Stores tab of Settings for the store the person works in. On the platform a super admin sees every account, and a platform user holding `user-view` sees store accounts only. Every WRITE endpoint resolves its target inside where the actor stands: `ResolvesCurrentStore::currentStore()` (404 unless the session's store exists and the actor is a member of it), `StoreTeam::roleOf()` (404 for somebody who is not a member), an invitation's `store_id` against the current store, a role through `Role::availableInStore`. Route middleware (`can:`) alone is NOT enough, and an id out of scope answers 404.

**Per-store isolation (the store is the wall):** whatever a member creates belongs to the store they created it in — stamped from `session('current_store_id')` on create — and every read and write of it lands in that same store. With no store selected a store member sees nothing at all (an empty scope, not an unfiltered list). When adding any new store-scoped entity: stamp it with the store, add the same walls, and add it to `Store::purgeContents()`, all in the same commit.

**Media library (store inventory, not personal property):** a library belongs to a shop, or to the platform (docs/CHANNEL-CONTENT-SPEC.md, owner 2026-09-19). Inside a store `media.store_id` is stamped from `session('current_store_id')` on upload — a store member with no store selected holds no permissions there and cannot upload at all, and the controller would refuse it anyway (422 with a message, never a silent global file). Above the stores the Media page reads ONE library at a time through its Library list — the platform's own (`store_id` NULL, `Media::platformOwned()`, files under `media/platform/`; the default) or a shop's (`/media/data?library=platform|{store}`) — and an upload joins the library chosen (`store_id` posted, or none for the platform's). NULL, never 0: the column's foreign key cascades, which is what makes a deleted shop take its files. `Media::visibleTo` scopes on the STORE ALONE, never on who uploaded: a media file is shared inventory that every colleague in the store must be able to put on a screen — and a plain `where('store_id', …)` never matches the platform's NULL rows, so a shop never lists, changes or plays one (a platform file reaches a television only inside a channel). Platform users span every library. Two consequences to preserve: the uploader leaving the store or deleting their account never touches the file — the menu must not vanish from the store's screens, so `created_by` goes NULL via the FK and the file stays; and files live on the `public` disk under `media/{store_id}/` (the platform's under `media/platform/`), with `MediaController::destroy` removing the row and both files (original + thumbnail) together — unless a channel shows the file: then the delete is refused naming the channels (`Media::stillInAChannelMessage`, "Still used by the channel … Take it out of that channel first."), the listing carries the same words (`in_channels_message`) so the page says it before any confirmation, and the same refusal stops an Ad Builder design whose published page a channel shows, before its password is asked. A playlist line is no reason to refuse: the file simply leaves those screens. **Ad Builder ads follow the industry's draft/publish model** (owner, 2026-09-21 — Xibo, Contentful, Strapi; docs/AD-BUILDER-SPEC.md §9): a save changes the draft only, and the screens keep the published page until **Publish changes** (`BuilderAd::hasUnpublishedChanges`, against the version Publish keeps in `published_document`/`published_name`); **Discard changes** goes back to that version; **Unpublish** clears `published_at`, and from then on the page is in no library or picker (`Media::scopeWithoutDrafts` — add it to any new list of media) and on no television, as a line, a channel's ad or a holding picture (`Media::isPlayableNow`, `ChannelAd::statusOn` 'draft') — while every line and channel ad holding it keeps its place, marked as a draft, and plays it again from the next Publish. **And an ad says WHERE it may play** (`builder_ads.in_playlists`, owner's rule 2026-09-22: "agar woh same ad channel mein hui aur playlist mein toh masla hoga"): the same ad inside a channel and on the playlist carrying that channel plays twice in one pass, so **an ad is for channels only until "Show in playlists" is ticked** in the editor's Publish ▾ menu — publishing does not tick it, and `builder:examples` does. Ticked, it joins the shop's own pickers (the playlist's and the holding picture's, both through `Media::scopeWithoutChannelOnly` — add it to any new list a SCREEN picks from, never to a channel's, which offers every published ad) and the walls behind them let it through (`PlaylistController::assertAdsMayBeOnAPlaylist`, `ScreenController::assertMayPlayByItself`, 422 naming the ad). Taking the tick off is refused while a screen still carries the ad, naming the screens (`Media::stillOnScreensMessage`) — nothing leaves a television behind somebody's back — and it never touches `updated_at`, so it is not a change the screens are waiting for. Thumbnails use GD (no image package); a video's poster frame, duration and dimensions are measured in the BROWSER at upload time and re-validated server-side, because there is no ffmpeg. Accepted formats (JPG, JPEG, PNG, GIF, WEBP, MP4, WEBM) live in `StoreMediaRequest::ALLOWED_MIMES` as the source of truth, and everything that mirrors it must change together: `ALLOWED_EXTENSIONS` in `resources/js/core/media-file.js` (which media-table, campaigns-table, channel-ads and builder-assets-table all import), the `accept` list plus helper text on each upload form (`media/index.blade.php`, `campaigns/index.blade.php`, `channels/show.blade.php`, `builder/assets.blade.php`), and the formats in words, `StoreMediaRequest::FORMATS_IN_WORDS`, which the refusal message of every upload request reads (`CampaignRequest`, `ChannelAdRequest` and the Ad Builder's `BuilderAssetRequest`, which also takes `ALLOWED_MIMES` itself). A still format also needs a reader in `MediaStorage::readImage`, or it uploads with no thumbnail.

**Screens & the device API (the one machine-facing surface):** a TV has no session, so `routes/device.php` is registered OUTSIDE the `web` group — no cookies, no CSRF, four endpoints only (`register`, `pair-status`, `playlist`, `heartbeat`). Deliberately NOT `routes/api.php`: the owner's no-API rule stands, this is a narrow device channel with its own stateless stack. Rules that must hold together: **open ≠ unauthenticated** — `register` is open but throttled 30/min per IP, `pair-status` is open but demands the `poll_secret` issued at registration (so a guessed uuid cannot steal a freshly minted token), and everything returning real content sits behind `device.token`. A screen's identity is a 64-char random token **stored only as a SHA-256 hash** and handed to the device exactly once, on its first successful poll, after which the `pairing_requests` row is destroyed. Pairing codes are 6 characters from an alphabet with no `I/O/0/1` (read off a TV across a room), live 15 minutes, and are created only when a player actually opens — so there is nothing to clean up but strays. `screens.store_id` follows the media wall: stamped from `session('current_store_id')` at pairing, `Screen::visibleTo` scopes on the store alone, and a screen from another store 404s. Re-pairing an existing screen rotates its token in place (old device locked out on its next request, playlist and settings kept) instead of creating a second row. **`DevicePairing::claim()` looks the row up INSIDE its transaction with `lockForUpdate()`** — a code answers to exactly one screen, and reading it unlocked let two people (two staff, two tabs, a retried request) both be told "screen added" while only one token can be stored, leaving the loser with a screen whose token nobody holds: permanently offline, and silent about why. Deleting a screen is how you revoke a device: it gets 401 and falls back to showing a fresh code. **Every throttle on this app uses a NAMED limiter (`AppServiceProvider::registerRateLimiters`), never the bare `throttle:n,1` form.** That form keys on domain+IP alone for a request with no logged-in user — the route is NOT part of the key — so every route using it shares one counter per visitor. A shop's TVs and the owner's laptop share one public IP, and each paired screen sends three requests a minute, so the bare form let a handful of screens exhaust the signup form's budget and lock a new TV out of pairing while the running screens hid the problem. The device limiters therefore count PER DEVICE (`device-api` by token hash, `device-pair` by `device_uuid`, each with a looser per-IP ceiling); only `device-register` keys on IP, because a screen asking for its first code has nothing else to be known by. `throttle:device-api` also runs BEFORE `device.token`, so a wrong token is rate limited too.

**Playlists (the thing a screen actually plays):** `playlist_items` is an ordered list of lines — each one a file `(media_id, duration_seconds)` or a channel `(channel_id)`, exactly one of the two (see **Channels**) — written by **replacing the whole list in one PUT** rather than patching positions — reorder, retime, add and remove are then the same call, it is atomic, and a half-finished rearrangement never reaches a TV. Three rules that must hold together: a screen may only play **its own store's** files (`PlaylistController::assertMediaBelongsToTheSameStore`, 422 otherwise — the store wall in the one place it is easy to forget); the **schedule is resolved server-side** in `DeviceController::playlist`, so a file outside its start/expiry window never reaches the device and a cheap box with a wrong clock still shows the right thing (the panel still lists it, greyed with its expiry); and every manifest item carries a **`checksum`** — a cache key derived from id+size+updated_at, not a content digest — so a future native shell can decide whether the copy it already has on disk is current. Reading a playlist is `screen-view`; WRITING one is `screen-playlist`, which also gates the picker's own media endpoint so it never drags in `media-view` (see **Capability-complete permissions**). Deleting a file cascades it off every screen it was playing on. **A save carries a `version`** — `Screen::playlistFingerprint()`, a content hash handed out by the GET and required by the PUT — and a mismatch is a **409**, never a silent overwrite. Because the PUT replaces the WHOLE list, a client working from a stale copy would otherwise erase whatever a colleague changed in between with nothing to show for it; the fingerprint is content-based so re-saving an identical list is not a conflict, and the PUT returns the NEW version so one page can save twice. Nothing polls for it — it travels with the save that was already happening, so this needs no websockets.

**Channels (content a shop may carry):** a channel (`channels` + `channel_ads`) is one of two kinds (owner's rules, 2026-09-16). The **platform's** channel (`store_id` NULL) is made ABOVE the stores — by the super admin, or a global role holding `channel-view/store/update/destroy` — and is offered to EVERY store, with no store targeting and no per-ad targeting. A **store's own** channel (`store_id` set) is made inside a store by a member whose role there carries the channel permissions, and is offered to that store's screens alone. A channel keeps no files of its own (docs/CHANNEL-CONTENT-SPEC.md, owner 2026-09-19): each ad holds a row of a media library by `channel_ads.media_id` (required, never a copy — publish an Ad Builder ad again and the channel shows the new page), named in the Add-ad form from the **Library**, the **Ad Builder**'s published ads (the same library's `html` rows) or a fresh **Upload** that joins the channel's library first — the shop's for a shop's channel, the platform's for the platform's. A shop's channel takes that shop's rows only; the platform's channel its own library or any shop's (`ChannelAdRequest::chosenMedia`, 422 otherwise); the pickers read `/channels/{channel}/library` under `channel-update`, never `media-view`, and taking an ad out leaves its file in the library. (Ads made before the change were carried into the libraries by a migration that named each file where it lay, so the old `channels/{id}/` shelf still holds theirs.) Five rules that hold together: (1) **reach** — every CHANGE goes through `Channel::visibleTo` (above the stores: every channel, each listed with whose screens it reaches; inside a store: that store's own — the platform's and another store's are 404, `ChannelAdController`'s controller middleware included, before an upload is even validated), and every LOOK through `Channel::listableIn`, which inside a store adds the platform's channels, listed and opened read-only (owner 2026-09-19: marked "From the platform", `read_only` on the row and `$readOnly` on the page, no Edit, Delete or Add ad, the screen counts of that store alone and no name of whoever on the platform made it) — another store's stays 404; a channel made inside a store is stamped with it, and a name need only stand apart within what one store's box lists — the platform's channels and that store's own (`ChannelRequest`; a store's name never blocks the platform's); (2) **the store wall on playlists** — a screen's Channels box and its saved lines take the platform's channels and the screen's own store's only (`Channel::availableTo`, `PlaylistController::assertChannelsAreAvailable`, 422 otherwise), and deleting a store deletes its own channels, their ads and the lines carrying them (`Store::purgeChannels`) — the files are the store's library, which `purgeMedia` takes, and with it, through `channel_ads.media_id`'s cascade, any ad of the platform's channel that showed one of them; (3) **the shop chooses** — a channel reaches a screen only as ONE playlist line (`channel_id` set, `media_id` and `duration_seconds` NULL) that a store user adds with the Add button in the Channels box, below Content library; the line plays at its position, carries its own schedule rules like a file, is copied with the playlist, and `Screen::playlistFingerprint` prefixes it so channel 7 never reads as file 7; (4) **resolved on the server** — `ScheduleResolver` sends a channel line only while the channel is active and has ads inside their dates on the SCREEN's local date; an UNSCHEDULED channel line rides along with the shop's own files (plays only while one is due, so it never lights up a screen the shop left dark) unless no file on the playlist is live at all, and a scheduled one follows its own rules; the device receives one entry `{type: channel, per_pass, ads[]}` and the PLAYER rotates `per_pass` (`buildPass` moves a channel's cursor only once a pass has really played); (5) **ownership** — whoever holds a channel permission may use it on ANY channel within reach (shared work, like a store's media), a channel outlives its creator (`created_by` goes NULL), and deleting a channel removes its ads and every playlist line carrying it — never a file, which stays in its library — with the panel showing the screen count first; pausing (`is_active`) is the way off the air without that. Seconds belong to IMAGES and Ad Builder pages only (at most `ChannelAd::MAX_IMAGE_SECONDS`); a video has no seconds field anywhere in the panel and runs to its measured end — the same rule the campaign form follows (owner's rule).

**Every email is drawn in the app's own shell (`resources/views/components/email/layout.blade.php`):** the two messages that leave the server — an invitation (`InvitationNotification`) and a password reset (`ResetPasswordNotification`, which `User::sendPasswordResetNotification` puts in place of Laravel's stock one) — are `->view(['emails.x', 'emails.x-text'], …)`: an HTML page and its plain-text twin, because a message with both parts is drawn by every client and trusted further by the filters in between. Email is not the panel: tables rather than flexbox (Outlook renders with Word's engine), every rule inline (Gmail strips `<style>`), one 600px column, `<x-email.button>` for the one action (a table cell, never an image), the brand bar in the panel's blue (#2563eb) on a white card that stays `#ffffff` so a client forcing dark mode tints it instead of leaving dark text on dark, and no `<img>` at all, so a blocked-images inbox still shows the whole message. Each one says who sent it, where the link leads, when it stops working and what to do if it was not asked for — the link is the only copy of its token. A new email adds its view pair, uses the layout, and is rendered in a test (`tests/Feature/System/EmailTemplatesTest.php`), never checked by eye alone.

**Capability-complete permissions:** a permission must be self-sufficient for its task. Option lists that feed an action come with the action's own module, returning only the minimal fields: the invite form's roles arrive with the team data (`/members/data` → `assignable_roles`), never through the Roles page's `role-view`; the playlist picker lists files through its own endpoint under `screen-playlist`, never `media-view`; a channel's Add-ad pickers read `/channels/{channel}/library` under `channel-update`, with only what a tile shows. Never make a feature depend on unrelated `-view` permissions of other modules.

**UI permission gating:** every action element (Add/Edit/Delete/Invite/Remove buttons, nav links) must be wrapped in `@can('<permission>')` matching its route's `can:` middleware — and, on a tier-locked route, its tier gate as well. What may be done to ONE row (an Owner's, your own) comes from the server with the row. The UI hides; the backend enforces. When adding any button, add its gate in the same commit.

**Settings (owner's rules, 2026-09-17):** the person's name at the foot of the sidebar (`dusk="sidebar-settings"`) and the header's user menu say "Settings" and open `/profile`. Settings is tabs (`<x-settings-tabs>`, one page per tab so every plain form returns where it was sent from): **Profile**, and **Stores** (`/settings/store`) — shown to a store member working in a store whose role there holds View Stores (`User::hasStoresTab()`; turning View Stores off hides it, the Owner role's included). There is no sidebar link to it, and a store's own people never see the platform's Stores page: a store is changed only from inside it, here. The Stores tab holds, each card by its own permission: Store Details (editable with `store-update`, read-only without), **Your stores** (every store the person belongs to with their role and Leave; Create store with `store-store` — a new store they own at once, a plain form whose fields carry a `store_` prefix so the details form never reads them back) and Delete Store (`store-destroy`, laid out like Delete Account on the Profile tab: the store's name typed, then the password). Without the tab, "Your stores" stays on the profile so anybody can still leave. A store changes hands on the Members page, not here. A settings page added later joins these tabs, not the sidebar.

**Role permission subsets:** a role's permissions are validated server-side in `RoleController::permissionsFor`: the catalogue (`Permission::SUPER_ADMIN_ONLY`) goes on no role but Super-Admin; a store role or a custom role carries only what can work inside one store (`Permission::belongsToStores`: `STORE` + `STORE_SCOPED`, message "A store role cannot hold …: it works above the stores only."); and a member inside a store gives a custom role only what they hold there themselves. The checklist lists ONLY what the role in the form can hold — never a greyed-out checkbox (owner's rule, 2026-09-17): `/roles/assignable` answers `?type=store|platform` for a new role on the platform (the form reloads it when the type changes and drops ticks the new type cannot hold), `?role=` for a role being edited, and inside a store what the member holds there that a store role can carry; groups that reach only the member's own store on a store role say "This store only". It is cosmetic; the server decides.

**Platform permissions (owner's rules, 2026-09-16: the super admin sees everything and decides who holds what; a store's people work within their store):** the lists live ONCE, on the model. `Permission::STORE` — a store's own work; on a platform role it reaches every store. `Permission::PLATFORM` (accounts, stores, channels, the activity log) — on a platform role it reaches every store. `Permission::STORE_SCOPED` — the platform permissions that also work inside one store, and on a store role or a custom role they reach THAT store alone: `store-view` (the Stores tab of Settings for the store they work in), `store-store` (Create store on that tab — a store they own at once, no invitation, and the active switch stays the platform's), `store-destroy` (Delete Store on that tab, for the store they work in — the Owner's by default), `channel-*` (the store's own channels, see **Channels**), `activity-view` (the store's own entries, see **Activity logging**). The store permissions act on the store the person works in and nothing else (Settings → Stores reads `ResolvesCurrentStore::currentStore()`); the platform's Stores page and its writes are `can:global-tier`. Never on a store's role: `user-view`/`user-destroy` (the accounts are the platform's alone — a store's people are its Members page; owner's rule 2026-09-17), `activity-destroy` (yearly maintenance drops every store's history at once), a permission made on the Permissions page, and the catalogue. `Permission::SUPER_ADMIN_ONLY` (`permission-*`) — the Super-Admin role alone, not even another platform role (owner's decision, kept). Every refusal holds TWICE, because a row can reach the wrong role by other means (written before the rule, or straight into the database): `RoleController::permissionsFor` refuses it (422), AND what only works above the stores keeps a tier lock on its route — `can:global-tier` on the Stores page and its writes, the accounts pages (`/users`), the activity log's partitions and maintenance, giving a store an owner, "Log in as" and the platform team; `can:super-admin-tier` on `/permissions`, platform invitations, platform-role removal and the Users page's Stores — putting a person in a store, changing that role, taking them out (`AppServiceProvider::registerTierGates`) — with the page and sidebar checking the same pair. *Why the catalogue is never delegated:* every permission name is written in code on the routes, so renaming a row breaks that feature for everybody but super admins (whom `Gate::before` lets through) or turns a harmless permission a person already holds into a powerful one. Campaigns (`campaign-manage`) are a hand-written super-admin gate, not a row, and stay so (owner's decision). **Adding a permission?** Put it in the right list with its label (`Permission::LABELS`) — and in `STORE_SCOPED` only once its controller answers a store's role for that store alone — ship a migration of its own that inserts it and grants it to Super-Admin (never edit the baseline `2026_09_16_110200_insert_permissions_and_starter_roles`, which is only what a fresh install starts with) — and, for a store permission the Owner role (and Admin, while the installation has it) should hold, to those roles by key (the seeder never touches an existing role) — and give its routes and nav link the right lock, all in the same commit. **Every new feature gets its permissions, granted to Super-Admin, and the super admin's (platform) side of it as well as the store's** (owner's rule, 2026-09-17: "super admin ko bhi uski permission dena aur uska sub banana"); the owner says when a permission should not reach the super admin.

**Blade gotcha — `@json` in an `x-data` attribute (caused a real bug):** `@json` encodes with NO flags, so its quotes stay raw and the first string in the payload closes the surrounding `x-data="…"` attribute — Alpine then receives half an expression and the whole component silently fails to initialise (`items is not defined`, an empty table, no error page). Booleans and ints are safe, which is why every earlier page got away with it. Anything carrying a string or an array must use **`{{ Js::from([...]) }}`** instead, which hex-escapes for exactly this position; pass the whole config object through one `Js::from` rather than mixing the two.

**Blade gotcha (caused a real bug):** `@error`/`@if` directives do NOT compile inside a Blade COMPONENT tag's attributes (`<x-text-input class="... @error(...) ... @enderror">` puts the literal text into the class list). Inside component attributes use echo syntax instead: `{{ $errors->has('field') ? '!border-red-500' : '' }}`. Directives are fine in plain HTML elements and inside component templates/slots.

**Blade gotcha — one `@php` form per file (caused a real bug):** Blade lifts `@php … @endphp` blocks out BEFORE anything else is compiled, matching from the first `@php` it finds — so a file that already uses the inline `@php($x = …)` form and then adds a block reads everything between the two as one PHP block, and every page including it dies with a parse error (the sidebar once took down every page). Keep to one form in a file, and never write those words inside a Blade comment either — comments are stripped after the blocks are lifted.

**One visual form language (every form, TWO tracks only):** required fields show a red `*` in the label; a field with an error gets a RED BORDER plus a red message directly below it. Tracks: (1) **AJAX/Alpine modals** → `x-crud.form-field` (wrapper `crud-field-error` paints the border, Alpine `formErrors` drives it); (2) **server-rendered plain-POST forms** (auth pages AND profile) → `x-auth.form-field` (supports `bag`, `value`, `id` props; error logic lives inside the component template). Any new form picks the track matching how it submits — never invent a third look, and never put error logic in a component-tag attribute (see Blade gotcha).

**Validation gotcha — a validated array's ORDER is not the client's order (caused a real bug):** `$request->validate()` rebuilds nested wildcard data RULE BY RULE, not row by row: for `items.*.media_id` it sets every row that HAS a media_id, then for `items.*.channel_id` every row that has one. A row missing the first key is therefore inserted into the result AFTER rows that have it, so `array_values($validated['items'])` reads a different order than the client sent — a playlist saved "channel first, poster second" reached the television the other way round, and only a browser test caught it (backend tests had sent the kinds in the lucky order). The keys are still the client's indexes, so anything that cares about order must `ksort()` first (see `PlaylistController::writeItems`) — never trust the array's own order when rows can have different shapes. For the same reason, ids are validated `min:1`: a posted `0` reads as "no id" to `filled()`/`->filter()` and would sail past the ownership checks into a foreign-key error.

**Alpine gotcha — no getters in `extraState`/`extraMethods` (caused a real bug):** `createCrudTable` merges both with object spread (`...extraMethods`), and spread READS a getter instead of copying the accessor. A `get allOnPageSelected() { return this.items.length … }` therefore runs at merge time, with `this` bound to the plain literal rather than the Alpine component — `this.items` is undefined, it throws, and the whole `xxxTable()` call dies before Alpine ever evaluates the `x-data`. The page then renders with an empty table and EVERY binding on it reporting "not defined", including bindings that have nothing to do with the new code. Write a plain method and call it with `()` from the view; getters are only safe in `crud-table-base.js`'s own object literal (see `endItem`), which is never spread.

**Big deletes re-confirm the password (owner's rule, 2026-09-16):** deleting a store (platform or Settings → Stores), an account (platform or Profile), a role, a permission, a channel, a campaign or an Ad Builder design, removing a member (Members page, or the platform's Users → Stores), and taking a platform role all ask for the actor's password; everyday deletes (a media file, a screen, a daypart, a channel ad, an Ad Builder asset, an invitation) stay one confirmation away. Changing your own password re-confirms it too. Server: `Concerns\ConfirmsPassword::confirmPassword($request, $bag, $field)` — one shared counter per person across all of these forms, JSON or plain (a plain form gets the error in its named bag) — called AFTER every other refusal so nobody types a password for something refused anyway, messages "Password is required." / "The password is incorrect.", and five wrong passwords a minute per person then a 429 ("Too many wrong passwords…"). UI: `<x-crud.password-confirm>` inside a real `<form>` (Chrome autofill), `deleteNeedsPassword` on `createCrudTable` or the page's own password state, the password error under its field. A new big delete adds both halves in the same commit.

**Frontend guards:** every AJAX action needs an in-flight flag (`saving`/`deleting`/...) with a disabled binding; plain forms are covered globally by `resources/js/core/form-guard.js`. Every form gets client-side validation via `resources/js/core/validate.js` mirroring the backend rules — the backend stays the source of truth.

**Deleting (owner's rules — nothing cascades through people):**
- **Remove a member / leave a store:** the membership row only. Their uploads, paired screens, playlists and custom roles stay with the store; `created_by` on content is history only (the FK goes NULL when an account is deleted) and nothing is ever deleted through it.
- **Delete an account** (Profile, or the platform's `user-destroy`): the account and its memberships — nothing else of anybody's — and nothing is left pointing at it (owner's rule, 2026-09-17): the memberships go through their foreign key, `created_by`/`invited_by`/`actor_id` empty (what the person made stays with its stores, and the log keeps their name), and the `deleted` hook in `User::booted()` removes their sessions on every device (database driver), their password-reset token and every invitation waiting for their email (`User::invitationsToEmail()` — to a store or to the platform, expired ones included; the deletion's log line counts them). Invitations the person SENT stay with their stores (`invited_by` empties). Self-deletion is refused while the person is the last Owner of any store (the message names the stores); a platform deletion goes ahead (the response names the stores left without an Owner) and the super admin gives them one with "Invite owner" on the Stores page or from Users → Stores. Super admins cannot delete themselves; deleting a platform account needs a super admin, and deleting a super admin the primary one. NO soft-delete for users, by explicit owner decision.
- **Delete a store** (`store-destroy`: inside a store — the Owner's by default — the store the person works in, from Settings → Stores; on the platform any store, from the Stores page — always typing the name and their password): everything the store owns goes, through the `deleting` hook in `Store::booted()` → `Store::purgeContents()` (BEFORE the row goes: the foreign keys would otherwise take the media rows first and leave their files with no row to name them) — memberships, invitations, custom roles, screens (their playlists and schedule rules with them; a device loses its token), dayparts, the store's own channels through `Store::purgeChannels()` (their ads and the lines carrying them; their files are library rows, next), the whole media library through `Store::purgeMedia()` (owner's rule, 2026-09-15): every row whoever uploaded it — and, through `channel_ads.media_id`'s cascade, any ad of the platform's channel that showed one of them — the rows inside the transaction and the files only after it commits (`DB::afterCommit`), and only the paths the rows name are unlinked — never a whole folder — and its Ad Builder designs and asset shelf through `Store::purgeBuilder()`, the same way (rows inside the transaction; each design's poster and each asset's files after commit, by the paths the rows name). **The store row goes too, for good** (owner's rule, 2026-09-17: "A to Z" — there is no soft delete and no `stores.deleted_at`), and `roles.store_id` cascades, so a custom role can never outlive its store and turn into a store role offered everywhere. The people's accounts stay, and so does the activity log: its entries keep the store's id, and its name in their words.

**Activity logging:** every mutation endpoint calls `ActivityLog::record()` (action like `member.invited`, subject, human description). When adding a new mutation endpoint, add its log line in the same commit. For actions performed while unauthenticated (e.g. password reset via email link), pass the acting user explicitly as `record()`'s fourth `$actor` argument. Deliberately NOT logged (owner-accepted noise/no-op exclusions): failed login attempts, forgot-password link requests, `confirm-password` (session-only), and the unused email-verification resend. **Which store an entry belongs to:** `activity_logs.store_id` — `record()` takes it from the subject (a store, or anything with a `store_id`: a screen, a media file, a daypart, a custom role, a store's channel, an invitation), or from its fifth argument `storeId:` wherever the subject is gone (a delete) or is a person (a member's role changed or removed). An account's own sign-in, profile or password and the platform's own work belong to no store; entries logged before the column existed have none. A new mutation endpoint inside a store makes sure its entry carries the store. **Who reads it (owner's rules, 2026-09-16):** above the stores the super admin and any global user given `activity-view` read every entry; inside a store a role carrying `activity-view` reads that store's entries alone (`ActivityLogController::data`), never another store's and never one without a store. Yearly maintenance (drops every year older than two, data included, for every store at once) is a permission of its own, `activity-destroy`, which only a platform role may carry: its routes and the partition status — which counts every store's rows — keep `can:global-tier`, and its panel renders for that pair only.

**Upload gotcha — `UploadedFile::fake()` believes the filename (weakens a real test):** a fake file's mime type comes from its NAME (`Illuminate\Http\Testing\File::getMimeType()`), while a real upload's comes from its BYTES (finfo). So `fake()->createWithContent('shell.jpg', '<?php …')` passes `mimes:jpg` in a test and is refused in production — the test proves nothing. To test what the server really does with a lie, build a real one: write the bytes to a temp file and `new UploadedFile($path, 'shell.jpg', 'image/jpeg', null, true)`, where the third argument is only what the CLIENT claims (`uploadWithBytes()` in `FileUploadAttackTest`). Keep `fake()` for size and for formats whose name and bytes agree.

**Input gotcha — a query parameter can be ANY shape (caused a real bug):** `?search[]=x` arrives as an array, and an array reaching a `like` clause, a `trim()` or a cache key is an "Array to string conversion" — which Laravel raises as an error, so the answer is a 500. It cost every listing in the panel and, worse, the device API's own limiter (`device-pair` keys on `?device_uuid`, and a limiter runs BEFORE the endpoint's validation, on a surface that is open on purpose). Wherever a word is read straight out of a request with no Form Request having checked its shape first, read it through `HandlesCrudData::plainValue()` — anything that is not one plain value reads as "not sent", which keeps a listing forgiving — or validate it (`['search' => ['nullable', 'string', 'max:255']]`, as both media pickers do).

**Validation gotcha — a later rule still runs after `string` failed (caused real bugs):** Laravel runs every rule for an attribute unless the list begins with `bail`. A `name` posted as an array therefore failed `string` and then reached the closure that folds the name to compare it — `(string) $value` on an array, a 500 (`RoleController::validated`, `ChannelRequest`); and a `password` posted as an array failed `string` and then reached `current_password`, which hashes whatever it is handed — a 500 on EVERY big delete in the app (`Concerns\ConfirmsPassword`). All three now begin with `bail`. Any rule after a type rule that assumes the type held — a closure, `current_password`, `exists`, a custom Rule object — needs `bail` in front of it; a closure that only compares with `!==` (the store-delete confirmations) is shape-proof and does not. The same applies one level down: a list validated as an `array` says nothing about what is INSIDE it, so a mapper typed `array $row` over `rules` blows up on `rules[]=x` until the elements are validated too (`PlaylistController::preview` now reuses the PUT's own `ruleRules()` for that).
